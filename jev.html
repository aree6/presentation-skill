Three question types cover the decisions software actually has to make

Mix all three in one call; each is evaluated independently.

One of your options

Choice

Up to 255 labelled keys. Returns the winner, a probability for each, and a confidence.

Key point: department: "technical", 0.85
A position on a scale

Score

A 2 to 10 level rubric you define. Returns a fractional score and the distribution over levels.

Key point: severity: 7.2 of 10, confidence 0.61
A number from 0 to 1

Noul

A calibrated probability from 0 to 1. No bare boolean to guess at.

Key point: is_urgent: 0.94, so page on-call
Ask four narrow questions, not one broad one, then weight them in code.

Training scores the probability against the outcome, not the wording against a rater

What RLHF rewards
Write-ups and chat responses that human raters prefer. A rater cannot tell you a probability was overconfident.
What RLCD rewards
Answers whose probabilities match outcomes. Saying 80% sure has to be right 80% of the time.
Optimising against outcomes is what makes an overconfident answer cost the model something.

The floor
0.5
Below this the model is telling you it does not know. Do not guess on its behalf.
The destructive bar
0.9
A read-only screen and an irreversible transfer get different thresholds.
The scale
0 to 1
1 when all the probability sits on one outcome, 0 when it is spread evenly.
A confidence score nobody has calibrated is a vibe with a decimal point on it.
